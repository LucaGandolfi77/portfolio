Quantum Tic-Tac-Toe - Complete Rules & FAQ

🎮 Game Overview

Quantum Tic-Tac-Toe is a variant of the classic game that introduces quantum mechanics concepts. Players alternate making moves, but can place either classical marks (permanent) or quantum marks (entangled pairs) that exist in superposition until they form a cycle and collapse.

📋 Official Rules (Based on Wikipedia)

1. Quantum Move (Entangled Move)
- Select 2 distinct empty cells on the board - Place a quantum mark that connects these two cells - The mark exists in superposition - both cells contain the mark until collapse - Cannot place on cells that already have classical marks

2. Classical Move
- Select 1 empty cell on the board - Place a classical mark (X or O) that is permanent - Cannot place on cells that contain quantum marks

3. Cycle Formation & Collapse
- When quantum moves create a closed loop (cycle), the board collapses - A cycle occurs when entangled marks form a connected loop - During collapse, each quantum move randomly chooses one of its two cells - All quantum marks in the connected component collapse simultaneously - The result is a classical board state with no quantum marks remaining

4. Winning Conditions
- Get 3 of your classical marks in a row (horizontally, vertically, or diagonally) - Quantum marks do NOT count toward winning - The game ends when a player wins or the board is full

5. Entanglement Rules
- Quantum marks are connected - they must collapse together as a unit - If one quantum mark in a connected component collapses, all connected marks collapse - Each quantum move affects the entire entangled component - The collapse process follows constraint propagation to ensure consistency

🎯 Game Flow

Turn Structure: Players alternate turns, starting with Player X.

Mode Selection: Choose between Quantum Move (entangled pairs) or Classic Move (single marks) before each turn.

Visual Indicators:

  • Classical marks show as large X/O symbols
  • Quantum marks show as small entangled symbols in each cell
  • Selected cells are highlighted with a glow effect
  • Cycle detection occurs automatically when quantum moves connect

📱 Mobile & Touch Controls

Touch Interface: The game is fully optimized for mobile devices with touch-friendly controls.

Gestures:

  • Tap a cell to select it (for quantum moves)
  • Drag between two empty cells for quantum moves
  • Tap a cell to place a classical mark
  • Use mode switch to toggle between Quantum and Classic moves

Responsive Design: The game adapts to different screen sizes and provides haptic feedback on supported devices.

💡 Strategy Tips

For Quantum Play:

  • Think ahead about potential cycles you might create
  • Consider the entire connected component when making moves
  • Quantum moves can be powerful for blocking opponents

For Classic Play:

  • Focus on creating winning lines
  • Block opponent's potential wins
  • Be careful not to leave cells that could become quantum traps

General Strategy:

  • Balance between quantum and classical moves
  • Control the center cell (position 4) when possible
  • Watch for opportunities to force cycles

🔧 Technical Details

Algorithm: The game uses cycle detection algorithms to identify when quantum moves form closed loops.

Collapse Logic: When a cycle is detected, the game randomly selects one cell per quantum move to collapse into, ensuring constraint propagation for consistency.

Visual Effects: The game features smooth animations, glow effects, and visual feedback for all game actions.

Performance: Optimized for smooth gameplay on both desktop and mobile devices with efficient rendering and touch handling.

❓ Common Questions

Q: Can I undo a quantum move?
A: No, quantum moves are final once placed. However, you can place classical marks to block or win.

Q: What happens if I create multiple cycles?
A: All quantum marks in the connected component collapse simultaneously, regardless of how many cycles exist.

Q: Is the collapse truly random?
A: Yes, each quantum move randomly chooses one of its two cells during collapse, ensuring fair gameplay.

Q: Can I play against an AI?
A: This version is player vs player. An AI opponent could be added in future updates.

Q: Why do quantum marks need to collapse together?
A: This follows the quantum mechanics principle of entanglement - connected quantum states must collapse as a unit.
